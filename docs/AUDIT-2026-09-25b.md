# Data and design audit, 25 September 2026

The second pass on the Cost and Delivery build. Everything below was measured
before anything was changed, and measured again after. Design system untouched:
every piece here is a component the build already had.

## What the audit found in the data

### 1. Every ratio was flat by construction

Measured across January to August 2026:

| | Capacity Utilization | SLA | Overtime rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PH Engineering | 1.39 points | 1.44 | 0.12 |
| PH Operations | 1.02 points | 1.11 | 0.17 |
| Ploceus | 0.82 points | 0.71 | 0.20 |

The generator drew one shared volume per day and let each key deviate 4% around
it, which pins every ratio to its annual value. That is why Penalty Exposure
drew a flat line, why the High-rate Resources bars were identical every month,
and why hero deltas read +0.03%.

### 2. Utilization of High-rate Resources was Effective Utilization

The site factors weighted to 1.0001, 1.0084 and 0.9990, so the metric returned:

| | The metric | Effective Utilization |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PH Engineering | 60.01% | 60.00% |
| PH Operations | 55.46% | 55.00% |
| Ploceus | 44.96% | 45.00% |

Two names, one number, on the same page. The title said rate, the breakdown
under it said location.

### 3. The locations said nothing

Bengaluru, Chennai and Hyderabad on all three vendors, factors between 0.96 and
1.08, so the breakdown showed six points of spread and Ploceus showed one site.
Nothing to consolidate, which is the metric's whole purpose.

### 4. The seat model was uniform, and structurally blind

A draw between a tenth and 2.15 times the mean put as many people at 6% as at
120%. Result: 27% of PH Engineering's roster over-utilized and 52% under, 79%
outside the healthy band. And because a seat was capped at 2.15 times the mean,
**Ploceus reported 0.00% over-utilized**: at 45% effective utilization the model
could not produce a single person over 100%. The worst-run vendor in the book
said nobody was overworked.

### 5. Two different things were called idle

Idle Capacity was 100 minus capacity utilization, 32% for PH Engineering. Idle
Hours was idle inside logged time, 14% of logged. Adjacent cards, same word.

## What the audit found in the design

Cost Efficiency was 3121px and Delivery 3404px against an 820px iPad viewport,
so roughly four screens each.

| Card | Height | What it was |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Partner Efficiency | 575px | two stat tiles, seven label-value rows, a chart |
| Leakage Breakdown | 572px | a headline, five rows with money sub-lines, a chart |
| SLA Risk Summary | 437px | six stacked project blocks |

On Consolidation Levers the axis was reported running past the card. It was not
reproducible at any width from 1024 to 2560, on any of the four dimensions, and
it corrected itself on a re-render. That points at a stale measured width
surviving a dropped ResizeObserver notification rather than a layout bug.

## What changed

### The data

**Personality.** Each vendor now travels an arc across the year, with a per-key
swing, so ratios move:

| | Capacity Utilization | SLA | Overtime |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PH Engineering, improving | 64 to 72 | 73 to 81 | 7.5 down to 6.6 |
| PH Operations, recovering then rolling back | 53 to 61 to 60 | 66 to 82 to 80 | 11.7 down to 8.9 |
| Ploceus, sliding | 46 down to 38 | 35 down to 25 | 12.8 up to 17.1 |

The calibrated year totals are untouched, so every figure pinned in 1.4 reads
exactly the same. Two things keep it safe, and both are asserted: calibration
scales each key to its configured year total whatever path it took, and keys
that are subsets of one another share a noise draw so their daily ratio moves
only along the arc. Without the second, a vendor already at a 94.7% output rate
posted days over 100%.

**Locations.** International, two vendors sharing hubs and one onshore niche:

| Vendor | Locations |
| --- | --- |
| PH Engineering | Bengaluru $62, Kraków $87, Manila $58, Belfast $115 |
| PH Operations | Manila $48, Bengaluru $51, Cebu $43, Guadalajara $86 |
| Ploceus | Zurich $94, London $83 |

Utilization spans 20 points inside a vendor. The weights and rate multipliers
are set so each vendor's blended rate stays its card rate.

**Utilization at High-rate Locations** now reads only the sites billing above
$65 an hour, so it is a different number and the one the name promises:

| | High-rate | Vendor average | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PH Engineering | 56.98% | 60.00% | its two expensive sites run 3 points behind |
| PH Operations | 41.80% | 55.00% | its one expensive site is its worst by 13 points |
| Ploceus | 44.92% | 45.00% | every seat it has is a high-rate seat |

**Seats** are a lognormal draw scattered per vendor: over 8.3 / 8.6 / 11.4 and
under 73.7 / 71.9 / 81.4. Every vendor can now show an overworked person, and
Ploceus has both the most overworked people and the most idle ones, which is
what badly distributed work looks like. Three quarters of the portfolio sitting
under 70% is the finding, not a defect: a book running at 56% effective
utilization against a 70% floor is under it by arithmetic, and that is the
pitch.

**Idle Capacity is now Unused Capacity.** Each tooltip says what it is not.

### The design

| Card | Was | Now | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Partner Efficiency | 575px | 469px | contract value as one bar: what the work you got cost, and what the work you did not get cost |
| Leakage Breakdown | 572px | 475px | one bar across contracted capacity: idle, non-core, non-billable against the productive remainder |
| SLA Risk Summary | 437px | 180px | one row per vendor, worst first, each project a chip, the reason one tap away |

Cost Efficiency is 2918px from 3121, Delivery 3146px from 3404.

The bars are the progress-bar idiom the location rows already use, split into
named parts. The chips are the badge pills the risk list already used, carrying
the project name instead of the words "At risk".

**Consolidation Levers.** Two structural changes rather than a guess at the
repro: `.chart` clips horizontally, so nothing can paint outside the card
whatever width the chart thinks it has, and `useMeasure` re-reads after every
render, so any change on the page corrects a stale value, which is what changing
the Compare by control was doing by accident. A third fix, reserving the
scrollbar gutter, was tried and reverted: it costs 15px, and 1180 minus the rail
minus 15 is under the 1100 the sheet needs, so the iPad would have rendered at
99.9% instead of 100%.

Also on that band: it named a vendor as the worst performer at a location it
does not staff. Only vendors present at a slice are compared now, and shared
locations sort first, so the comparable work is on the left where the eye starts.

## Verification

- `test:engine`: 26 figures per vendor, five portfolio targets, four identities,
  every day of 2026 walked for missing series and utilization over 100, every
  day of eleven years walked for a subset escaping its set, each vendor's arc in
  the direction it was given, high-rate utilization distinct from effective
  utilization, and every vendor able to show an overworked person.
- `test:charts`: axis ladder and column geometry.
- `test:ui`: 53 Playwright tests.
- A 255-state sweep across four lenses, every period, every filter: no empty
  state, no NaN, no percentage over 100.
- Geometry at 1180 and 1720: zoom 1, no horizontal scroll, hero in one row on
  one baseline, 11px axis labels, zero scaled charts.
